1922 to 1984

Arnold Mikelson

Sculptor, designer and teacher. He carved from the age of four, and he built this gallery with his own hands.

A carver from the start

Arnold was born in 1922 in Rauna, Latvia, the son of a cabinetmaker. A visiting aunt noticed him carving wood when he was four years old.

He studied crafts until he was sixteen and trained in mechanical engineering. At seventeen he received a Gold Medal in Riga.

Germany and England

When the Soviet army invaded Latvia in 1940, Arnold fled to Germany. For seven years he led a workshop of around forty artisans, finishing their wooden pieces with his carving. Many German churches still hang his carved chandeliers.

In 1947 he moved to England and became Chief Designer at Royal Crown Derby Porcelain, where he created three-dimensional sculptures including the Chelsea Birds.

Canada, and a gallery of his own

In 1954, Senator Donald Cameron of the Banff School of Fine Arts saw Arnold's work and offered him a position. Arnold came to Canada and spent about eleven years in Alberta as an architectural draftsman, designing schools, hospitals and homes.

In 1964 he settled in the Lower Mainland of British Columbia and began building the Mind & Matter Gallery. It opened in 1966, and he carved, taught and exhibited there for the rest of his life.

Arnold Mikelson's guiding idea

His work

Arnold worked mainly in wood, and also in stone, metal and precious-metal jewellery. He was best known for his birds.

  1. 1971

    Three sculptures for the Amy Ryan Fine Arts Center in Abilene, Texas: Roadrunner, Mockingbirds and Inspiration.

  2. 1976

    Gold and silver medals at the International Carvers Association show at the Canadian National Exhibition, where his work won eleven of fifteen categories.

  3. Commissions

    Work for MacMillan Bloedel, the Province of British Columbia and the City of Surrey, and a fundraising effort for Southwell Minster in England.

  4. Teaching

    He taught in local schools, especially young children, and was featured in Fine Woodworking, Beautiful British Columbia, newspapers and broadcasts.

  5. 1984

    Arnold passed away on February 9, 1984. Mary has kept the gallery and his work open to the public ever since.
